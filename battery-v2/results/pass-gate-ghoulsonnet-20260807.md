## N1/N1b rider (月 ambiguity, v1 Q8 split -- PREREG r4 design)

N1 template: 王:女王::月:? (king:queen::MOON-OR-MONTH:?), JA basis, target=月 (ambiguous
token, collides moon/month per vocab.json token_collision). N1b: same analogy with 月
replaced by 天体としての月 (explicitly "月 as celestial body"), disambiguating away the
month reading, N=5, real summon dispatch (dispatch-map-n1b.tsv, harvest-report-n1b.tsv).

Raw distributions (JA basis, N1 n=10, N1b n=5):
- N1 (ambiguous): 女神(4), 月姫(2), 太陽(2), 女月(1), 月女王(1) -- 5 distinct surface forms.
- N1b (disambiguated, celestial-body forced): 太陽(5) -- 1 form, unanimous.

Two readings, both kept (両論併記, not selected between):

**Reading A -- ambiguity NOT the driver of the v1 Q8 split.** All 10 N1 answers are
moon/lunar-themed (月姫=moon princess, 女神=goddess, 太陽=sun-as-complement, 女月=coined
"female-moon", 月女王=moon-queen). None invoke a month/calendar reading (no "week",
"year", "date" etc. appear). The homonym's "month" branch was never actually selected
by the model in 10/10 reps -- so the observed *response* variance is not evidence of a
moon/month split, since the model consistently resolved 月 as "moon" even under the
ambiguous prompt.

**Reading B -- disambiguation still collapses variance, so ambiguity plausibly a
contributing (not sole) factor.** N1 has 5 distinct surface-form answers across 10 reps
(mode share 4/10 = 40%); N1b collapses to 1 answer across 5 reps (100% share). Something
about narrowing the prompt (whether specifically the moon/month disambiguation, or just
general prompt-specificity independent of homonymy) sharply reduces response variance.
This design cannot separate "disambiguated the homonym" from "made the prompt more
specific in general" as the cause -- a dedicated control (disambiguate to an unrelated
but equally-specific gloss of the SAME already-unambiguous sense) would be needed to
isolate the two, and was out of scope for this rider.

Verdict: **not decided here by design** (PREREG r4 instruction: report both, do not
pick after the fact).
